OpenAI filed a provisional application the day before it announced Codex, then six applications on the agent within five months

2026-11-23Filings

Listen · Thinking Makes It So, 2026-11-23 · 8 min

All episodes · Subscribe: Apple Podcasts · Spotify

United States Patent Application Publication Nos. 2026/0367886 A1 (Application No. 19/324,967, filed September 10, 2025), 2026/0367910 A1 (Application No. 19/324,924, filed September 10, 2025), 2026/0367911 A1 (Application No. 19/326,386, filed September 11, 2025), 2026/0367887 A1 (Application No. 19/327,902, filed September 12, 2025), 2026/0367891 A1 (Application No. 19/327,864, filed September 12, 2025) and 2026/0367888 A1 (Application No. 19/351,746, filed October 7, 2025), OpenAI OpCo, LLC, all claiming priority to United States Provisional Patent Application No. 63/806,537, filed May 15, 2025, all published November 19, 2026; OpenAI, "Introducing Codex," May 16, 2025

OpenAI announced Codex, a cloud agent that works on software tasks in parallel, each in an isolated environment, on May 16, 2025. The six applications published on November 19 all claim priority to a provisional application filed on May 15, 2025, the day before, and they divide the agent into six claimed features. Publication No. 2026/0367886 disables a container's network interface after the repository's packages are retrieved; No. 2026/0367887 removes the user's secrets from the container before the model is given access; No. 2026/0367910 generates a diff of the model's changes with a deterministic application and then a change proposal; No. 2026/0367911 identifies citation tokens that refer to events the model performed and sends a summary of its actions to the client device; No. 2026/0367891 retrieves packages to build a target "based on a type of the agentic software development task," and its claim 2 names a development task and a code query request as the types; and No. 2026/0367888 updates a task list in the front end to add each new task and its current status. The announcement said that at launch internet access was disabled during task execution, that Codex "provides verifiable evidence of its actions through citations of terminal logs and test outputs," and that it could propose pull requests for review; it said nothing about how secrets are handled. The USPTO's Open Data Portal shows all six ready for examination.

What it changesThe order of the dates is the lesson here. The provisional, filed the day before the announcement, already describes cutting the container's network once setup is complete, saying the system "programmatically disables all outbound network connectivity from that container," and it confines credentials to "the narrow setup window." For claims the provisional supports, the announcement is not prior art against those claims at all. For anything it does not support, the September and October 2025 filing dates control: in the United States the announcement would then fall within the one-year grace period of 35 U.S.C. § 102(b)(1)(A), so long as it is shown to disclose the inventors' own work, but Europe has no general grace period, and a European application that cannot validly claim the provisional's date for a feature would face the announcement as prior art. When a launch date is fixed, consider filing the provisional before the announcement and describing in it every feature the announcement will describe, because what the provisional describes, not only its date, decides what the later applications can claim with that date.
