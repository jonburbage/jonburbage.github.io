OpenAI filed a continuation on generating and editing apps inside another application before the parent patent issued

2026-11-16Filings

Listen · Thinking Makes It So, 2026-11-16 · 9 min

All episodes · Subscribe: Apple Podcasts · Spotify

United States Patent Application Publication No. 2026/0349689 A1, OpenAI OpCo, LLC, "Online Development Environment Using Generative Response Engine," Application No. 19/706,496, filed June 12, 2026, published November 5, 2026, a continuation of Application No. 19/303,790, filed August 19, 2025 and issued as United States Patent No. 12,675,262 on July 7, 2026, which claims priority to United States Provisional Patent Application No. 63/800,246, filed May 5, 2025

Claim 1 receives a prompt in an outer application to generate an inner application, obtains code that includes at least one function corresponding to the prompt's natural language description, executes it, and renders the inner application's user interface in the outer application's main area. It then determines a modification request from user input or from an agentic action generated by the model, obtains modification instructions from the model, applies them and updates the interface. Claim 2 makes the instructions a patch to a module, claim 3 adds a dependency graph relating that module to others, and claim 4 has the patch change at least one of source code, styling information, a configuration file, a dependency manifest or package metadata. The USPTO's file history shows the parent examined under Track One prioritized examination, allowed on March 10, 2026, and issued as Patent No. 12,675,262 less than eleven months after filing, with no pre-grant publication listed; this continuation was filed two days after the parent's issue fee was received and twenty-five days before the parent issued.

What it changesThe sequence matters more than any one claim. OpenAI obtained the parent patent quickly through prioritized examination and kept the family open with a continuation filed before the parent issued, so it can pursue claims different from the parent's while keeping the May 5, 2025 priority date for whatever the provisional supports. Claim 1 is broad: it covers generating, running and modifying an app from a prompt without saying how the modification is made, and I expect the examiner to look hard for earlier systems that generated and ran an application from a prompt; the patch with a dependency graph in claims 2 and 3 is the narrower position to fall back on. When an allowance arrives on a family that covers a product you ship, decide on a continuation while the parent is still pending; it must be filed before the parent issues, and filing before the issue fee is paid leaves the most room.
