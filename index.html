The problem the firm is built around

Eligibility is where artificial intelligence patents are won or lost.

Two things are true at once right now. The USPTO has moved to make software and machine learning claims easier to allow: the Director’s August 2025 memorandum narrowed the mental-process rejection and put examiners to a preponderance-of-the-evidence standard, and the Appeals Review Panel decision in Ex parte Desjardins treated efficiency gains recited in a claim as a practical application.

The Federal Circuit has moved the other way. Recentive Analytics v. Fox, Rensselaer v. Amazon and Dental Monitoring v. Align all hold that applying a known model to a new data environment is abstract, and that better accuracy is not a technical improvement.

An application drafted only to get allowed is not necessarily an application that survives. The work this firm does is drafting one specification that satisfies both standards, and deciding, matter by matter, which fights are worth having in front of the examiner and which belong on appeal.

The claim has to recite the technical mechanism, not the result the mechanism produces. That decision is made at drafting, years before anyone tests it.
